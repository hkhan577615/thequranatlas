Ring structure

النظم الحلقي

Some scholars read surahs as rings: sections that mirror each other around a centre, A B C … C′ B′ A′. Below is one such reading of Al-Baqarah, a vocabulary check of it, and an echo map you can run on any surah.

Al-Baqarah as a ring

Raymond Farrin divides the surah's 286 verses into nine sections that pair up around a centre (Surat al-Baqara: A Structural Analysis, 2010; Structure and Qur'anic Interpretation, 2014). Arcs join each section to its mirror. Tap a section to read its first verse.

    Does the vocabulary agree?

    Each square compares the roots used in two of Farrin's sections, weighting rare roots more than common ones. Outlined squares are the mirrored pairs. A ring built on themes need not show up in word counts, so this is a check, not a verdict.

          Voice

          الالتفات

          Who is speaking, and to whom? Arabic marks person on every verb and pronoun, so each verse can be read for its I and We, its You, and its He, She and They. The Quran turns between them often, a device the rhetoricians called iltifāt.
